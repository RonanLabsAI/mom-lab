## Design decisions after Muse + Codex review (logs/consults/*-design-q1.md)
- Initialisation: the reviewers split (Muse: Base primary; Codex: post-trained primary). Both biases favour the pooled model (Base: pooled sees 6x the chat-format data; post-trained: pooled multi-task protects against forgetting), so a specialist win under either is conservative. Training at 1.7B is cheap, so we run BOTH: post-trained Qwen3-1.7B (headline, practical) and Qwen3-1.7B-Base (ablation).
- Fairness: equal aggregate exposure. Each specialist N tokens of its domain; pooled gets the exact multiset of the six specialists' examples (6N), interleaved. LR swept {5e-6, 1e-5, 2e-5} separately for specialists and pooled on dev; each arm gets its best dev LR.
- Recipe (start): full fine-tune, AdamW b2 0.95, wd 0.01, clip 1.0, 3% warmup, cosine, 2 passes, 65,536 tokens/update, max len 8192, assistant-only loss, no packing (length-grouped batches; no cross-example attention by construction). Own training loop (train/train.py), not TRL, for transparency; no FlashAttention available on these Blackwell chips, SDPA used.
- Added baselines (both reviewers): domain-tagged pooled (non-negotiable), random-partition specialists (does semantic splitting matter?), TIES/average merge of specialists, random routing, specialist x domain transfer matrix, LoRA library, distillation, equal-memory single models. Token-level MoE upcycling: out of scope for now.

- Seeds: 3 seeds for headline arms in development (oracle-routed library vs pooled vs tagged-pooled); 1 seed for exploratory sweeps; 5 for confirmation (Part B).
